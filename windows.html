Which Window Pays?

Every option below was played through all 28 games the bot camped, Nov 21–23 — the way you asked: the first time a game's price hit our level inside that option's window, the bet is booked at the real price showing at that moment. A win pays at that price, a push returns the stake, a loss costs the stake. Ranked by raw total profit. Nothing else counts.

The lines, in plain words. The game already has T goals when we camp it. T+0.5 = one more goal wins, nothing back otherwise. T+0.75 = a split line — one more goal wins half, two win it all. T+1.0 = one more goal gives the stake back, two more win. Our trigger: the price must reach 1.65. Dollars are shown at today's stake, $6.19 a bet.
What actually happened with real money: all 7 real bets were the T+1.0 line — 2 wins, 1 loss, 4 pushes — and they netted +$45.78 (wins +$63.87, the one loss −$18.09, pushes returned). The simulation below is the same math, run over every game we camped, not just the ones we bet.

The honest limits

Profit totals cover the graded games only. Final scores are in our records for 9 of the 28 games; the other 19 are being pulled now. Trigger counts are over all 28. When the finals land, every option gets re-run over the full history and this page's totals get replaced — Options 2 and 3 in particular could move a lot.

Small numbers. The top option's profit rests on 6 graded games; the bottom one's on 2. Raw profit is the right yardstick — it's just a young yardstick.

Soft clock. The recorded match minute can lag the real clock by about a minute, so the window edges are ±1' soft.
The one decision this raises: do we keep camps watching into 67–75' on games we haven't bet — just to build the missing data — or keep ending camps early the way we do now? Everything above is measurement; that choice is yours.
